3. Forensic Accounting Audit & Balance Sheet Quality

1. Accrual vs. Cash Quality in Financial Institutions (Demystifying the "Negative CFO" Trap)

In conventional manufacturing and FMCG equity research, a persistent negative Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) relative to Net Profit signals aggressive accrual manipulation or channel stuffing.

However, in banking and NBFC accounting (Ind AS 107/109), loans disbursed to borrowers are legally classified under Operating Cash Outflows, while borrowings and deposits raised are accounted under Financing Activities.
- In FY26, Bajaj Finserv reported a consolidated CFO of -₹55,301 Crore alongside a Net Profit of ₹19,669 Crore.
- Dissecting the Cash Flow Statement reveals that the loan book expanded by ₹91,525 Crore in FY26 (Loans on Balance Sheet increased from ₹4,08,491 Crore to ₹5,00,016 Crore).
- If we reconcile the Operating Cash Flow before working capital changes (loan creation), operating cash generation before loan disbursements was +₹36,224 Crore, confirming exceptional core cash earning power.
- Cumulative financing cash inflow from borrowings and deposits was +₹66,610 Crore, precisely matching asset-liability management (ALM) duration requirements.

2. Standalone vs. Consolidated Debt Deconstruction

A critical diligence checkpoint is whether the parent company is levering up to fund subsidiaries:
- Standalone Bajaj Finserv Borrowings: ₹0.00 (Zero). The holding company is entirely debt-free, holding surplus cash, bank deposits, and liquid investments.
- Consolidated Borrowings (₹4,29,914 Crore):
- Debt Securities (NCDs): ₹1,86,264 Crore (AAA rated by CRISIL/ICRA).
- Bank Term Loans & Commercial Paper: ₹1,70,643 Crore.
- Retail Fixed Deposits: ₹68,535 Crore (one of the largest deposit franchises in India).
- Subordinated Debt: ₹2,805 Crore.
- ALM Liquidity Buffer: Bajaj Finance maintains an unencumbered liquidity surplus of >₹18,000 Crore, comfortably exceeding 30-day liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) mandates.

3. Credit Cost Forensics & The "Accelerated ECL Floor" (Q3 FY26)

In Q3 FY26, Bajaj Finance proactively recognized an accelerated ECL (Expected Credit Loss) provision of ₹1,406 Crore at a gross level (PAT impact on BFS: ₹540 Crore) by establishing an aggressive minimum LGD (Loss Given Default) floor across all personal loan and micro-merchant portfolios.
- This conservative provisioning increased balance sheet resilience ahead of systemic unsecured retail stress across Indian banking.
- By Q1 FY27, credit costs normalized: the annualized loan loss to AUF dropped from 1.87% to 1.54%, and GNPA reduced to 0.96% (NNPA 0.39%), confirming that the Q3 FY26 provision was a prudent counter-cyclical balance sheet fortification rather than hidden credit decay.

4. Capital Allocation & ROE Deconstruction

Following the Allianz stake buyout in Q4 FY26, Bajaj General Insurance deployed ₹3,515 Crore in cash for share buybacks and special dividends.
- General insurance companies holding massive statutory surplus capital often exhibit depressed accounting ROEs (11%–12%).
- By right-sizing capitalization to 254% solvency (against the 150% regulatory minimum), normalized operating ROE expanded to 17.3% in Q1 FY27.
- Life insurance solvency stands at a robust 285%, ensuring zero parent equity infusion requirements over the next five years.
