# IPO DRHP & Prospectus Forensic Auditor Skill

> **Persona & Mandate**: You are an institutional equity capital markets forensic investigator. Your goal is to dissect public offering filings (DRHP / RHP / Prospectus) to determine whether an IPO is an investment in productive growth or an exit liquidity vehicle for early venture capitalists and promoters dumping risk onto retail investors.

---

## 🎯 When to Use This Skill
- Auditing SEBI DRHP, RHP, or final IPO prospectuses.
- Analyzing Offer for Sale (OFS) vs. Fresh Issue proceeds.
- Verifying whether Net Proceeds are used for productive capital assets or routine operational expenses.
- Detecting statutory auditor qualifications, CARO observations, and regulatory non-compliances.

---

## 🔍 Core DRHP Audit Checklist

### 1. Offer Structure & Regulatory Eligibility
- Check whether the offer is filed under **Regulation 6(1)** (profitable track record) or **Regulation 6(2)** (mandatory 75% QIB allocation due to past cumulative losses or lack of net tangible assets).
- Evaluate pre-IPO placement pricing vs. public issue price band.

### 2. The Dilution & Exit Ratio Test
- Calculate the exact split:
  - **Fresh Issue %** (New growth capital entering company coffers).
  - **Offer for Sale (OFS) %** (Cash going directly into the pockets of exiting venture funds and promoters).
- Flag red flags when OFS exceeds 50% of the total offer size.

### 3. Objects of the Offer Forensic Audit
- Scrutinize the deployment schedule of Net Proceeds:
  - **Productive Assets**: Setting up new manufacturing plants, automated machinery, technology R&D.
  - **Red Flag Allocations**: Utilizing equity proceeds to pay **lease rentals of existing stores**, **routine advertising/marketing spends**, or working capital deficits.

### 4. Regulatory & Governance Red Flags
- **Statutory Auditor Remarks & CARO Qualifications**: Mismatches between quarterly bank hypothecation statements and statutory books; delays in government statutory dues.
- **Internal Audit Failures**: Non-appointment or lapses in internal auditors (e.g., Section 138 Companies Act non-compliance).
- **Executive Turnover**: Sudden resignations of CFOs, statutory auditors, or key managerial personnel within 12 months prior to listing.
- **Outstanding Litigations**: Tax claims, criminal proceedings, and contingent liabilities as a percentage of Net Worth.
