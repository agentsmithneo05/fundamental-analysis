## 1. Core Lecture Discussion & Concepts

In this session, Parth Verma systematically deconstructs the foundational and operational mechanics of **Market Cap Definitions & Core Equity Categories (Large, Mid & Small Cap)**. 

### Primary Themes Addressed by the Instructor:
- **SEBI Market Capitalization Definition**: AMCs cannot arbitrarily decide what constitutes a large or small cap stock. SEBI and AMFI publish a standardized list every six months
- **- Large Cap**: Top 100 companies by market capitalization on Indian exchanges.
- **- Mid Cap**: 101st to 250th companies by market capitalization.
- **- Small Cap**: 251st company onwards.
- **Large Cap Funds**: Mandated to invest at least 80% of total assets in top 100 stocks. Blue-chip stability, steady cash flows, lower drawdown during market crashes, but limited multi-bagger upside.
- **Mid Cap Funds**: Mandated to invest at least 65% in 101st-250th stocks. Emerging sector leaders, higher growth runway, higher beta volatility.
- **Small Cap Funds**: Mandated to invest at least 65% in 251st+ stocks. High growth potential, business model fragility, severe liquidity risk during bear markets.

## 2. Real-World Context & Detailed Scenarios

To translate the lecture discussion into actionable investor understanding, consider the following practical scenarios and industry mechanics:

- Market Cap Spectrum in India: Large Caps (Reliance, TCS, HDFC Bank, Infosys) represent over 68% of India's total market cap. Mid Caps (Tata Communications, Polycab, Persistent Systems) offer proven business models expanding nationwide. Small Caps represent niche businesses or micro-caps.
- Drawdown Scenarios across 2008 & 2020: During the 2008 financial crisis, Nifty 50 (Large Cap) fell ~55%, while the Nifty Smallcap Index crashed ~78%. During the 2020-2021 bull run, Small Cap funds rallied over 150%, demonstrating the high-beta asymmetry.

## 3. Practical Investor Takeaways

1. Large Cap funds offer stability and inflation-hedging returns (11-13% expected CAGR over long horizons).
2. Small Cap funds require an investment horizon of 7+ years and a high tolerance for 30-40% intermediate drawdowns.
